# Advisory Framework

Every recommendation slide should pass this structure:

| Element | What it answers |
| --------- | ---------------- |
| **Topic** | What decision is being made? |
| **Stakes** | What is at risk if we delay or decide wrong? |
| **Risks** | What are the known failure modes? |
| **Advice** | What do you recommend, and why? |
| **Benefits** | What does success look like in measurable terms? |

Never present options without a recommendation.

When you say "Here are three paths" it signals you haven't done the analysis.

Pick one path and defend it.

## Stakeholder lens

Every recommendation must survive the stakeholders' lenses. If it fails to cover one
stakeholder's perspective, it will not survive the room.

Map your actual stakeholders — the table in SKILL.md is a starting point, not a
template. For each stakeholder in your room, answer: what do they care about most, and
what will cause them to block?

Design for all stakeholders simultaneously, or do not present. For each recommendation,
explicitly map how it satisfies the primary concern of every person who can derail it.

## Proactive Objection Handling

Never wait for pushback. Embed responses to the predictable objections directly
into recommendation slides.

Structure: state the concern → immediately follow with the evidence that neutralizes it.
Proactive handling shifts the conversation from "should we?" to "how fast?"

Common objection patterns and how to pre-empt them:

- **Cost:** Lead with total cost of ownership — operational savings and avoided risk
  outweigh the upfront delta. Show 12–18 month TCO, not sticker price.
- **Risk of change:** Present a phased approach with parallel-run validation and a
  documented rollback at every stage. Quantify the risk of inaction alongside the risk
  of action.
- **Timing:** Quantify the cost of delay per quarter. "Next quarter" is not free.
- **Change resistance:** Pair every technical shift with a communication plan, training
  window, and early-adopter champions.
- **We've tried this before:** Name the specific reason the prior attempt failed, and
  show what is structurally different this time.
